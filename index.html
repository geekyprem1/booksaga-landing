So Why Isn't Everyone Publishing Series?

Because until now, writing one was an absolute nightmare.

A series isn't five random books. It's five books that must agree with each other on every single detail:

✕Same characters behaving consistently across 500+ pages
✕A timeline that doesn't collapse in Book 4
✕Tracking names, eye colors & secrets in messy notes
✕One big macro arc while each book still resolves its plot
✕Endings in every book that make the next one unskippable
✕Matching professional covers that look like a family
Ghostwriters charge $2,000+ per book. For a 5-book series, that's a $10,000 bet before you've earned a single dime!

CHAPTER 1: THE NOTE IN THE VERSE

The lakeside bell chimed twice when Margaret Finch unlocked the worn pine door of Lakeside Books. It was seven in the morning, and the autumn mist still clung to Lake Willow like a shroud of damp gauze. She had run this quiet shop for twenty-two years, ever since Arthur had passed, but this morning felt distinctively, dangerously unsettled.

A fresh crate of donated estate books had been left on the porch overnight, sheltered beneath the green canvas awning. Margaret pulled on her canvas work apron, poured a mug of black coffee, and knelt beside the wooden box.

Near the middle of the stack lay a first edition of Victorian poetry, its leather spine cracked and dusty. As Margaret slid the volume free, a folded cream envelope slid out from between pages 140 and 141. No name. No postage stamp. Just four words penned in faded black fountain ink across the crease:

"He didn't drown accidentally."

Margaret's breath caught in her throat. Her mug rattled against the oak counter as she set it down. Arthur's rowboat had been found overturned eleven years ago in the deepest trench of Lake Willow. The county coroner had ruled it an accidental drowning within forty-eight hours.

She brought the note closer to the morning window light. In the lower right corner was an embossed seal of the Willow Creek Municipal Archives — and beneath it, in shaky but unmistakable cursive, were her husband's exact initials: A. F.
